Cosmic Paradoxes

A paradox in physics is rarely a contradiction in nature. It is a spot where an innocent assumption, like "now is the same everywhere" or "the universe is the same forever", quietly fails. Each page here lets you poke at one and find the assumption.

Every page has a short story, a hands-on demo, and a collapsed Go deeper box with the equations. You can skip the boxes and still follow along.

1 · Relativity

The Twin Paradox

One twin flies away and comes back younger. Why isn't it symmetric? Scrub through the trip on a spacetime diagram and watch the "now" line jump.

 2 · Cosmology

Why Is the Night Dark?

If stars go on forever, every line of sight ends on one, and the sky should glow. Fill up the sky shell by shell.

 3 · Life

The Fermi Paradox

Billions of planets, billions of years, and no visitors. Play with the Drake equation and see how a few guesses decide everything.

 4 · Relativity

The Barn and the Pole

A pole too long for a barn, squeezed in by length contraction. Is it inside or not? Depends who you ask, and when.

 5 · Expansion

Faster Than Light, Sort Of

Distant galaxies recede faster than light, yet nothing breaks the speed limit. Drag a galaxy out past the horizons.

Honest caveats

  • These are teaching toys. Numbers are rounded, and the cosmology uses standard textbook values (a Hubble constant near 70 km/s/Mpc).
  • Some of these are resolved (twins, barn, Olbers, horizon) and one is genuinely open (Fermi). The pages say which is which.
  • Wikipedia is linked at the bottom of each page as a next stop.
