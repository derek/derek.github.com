The Barn and the Pole

A 20 m pole runs at high speed toward a 10 m barn. Moving things get shorter, so to the farmer the pole shrinks until it fits, and both doors can be shut with the pole inside. But to the runner, the barn shrinks, and the pole is even more hopelessly too long. Which is it?

Both are correct. They disagree on one thing: whether the two doors shut at the same moment. The farmer says yes. The runner says no, and that is enough to make both stories fit.

Two views of the same event

Farmer's view: the barn is still, the pole is moving and short.

Runner's view: the pole is still and long, the barn is moving and short.

Set a speed that makes the pole fit (the demo tells you the minimum) and scrub the farmer's clock. Both doors flash shut at once while the pole is wholly inside. Now scrub the runner's clock. The far door shuts and reopens first, before the pole's front reaches it, and only later does the near door shut, after the pole's tail has gone through. The runner never sees the pole inside the barn with both doors shut.

Moral: "at the same time, in two different places" is not a fact about the world. It is a fact about a frame. Length contraction and the relativity of simultaneity are the same thing viewed two ways, and the paradox lives where you assumed they weren't.

Go deeper: the Lorentz transformation

With c = 1, γ = 1/√(1−v²), the pole of rest length L₀ = 20 m has length L₀/γ in the barn frame, which is less than the barn length B = 10 m when γ > 2, i.e. v > 0.866c.

Events: both doors close at barn time tc: (x, t) = (0, tc) and (B, tc). In the pole frame t' = γ(t − vx), so the near door closes at γtc and the far door at γ(tc − vB), earlier by γvB.

With the doors kept shut only while the pole is fully inside (a window of γ(B − L₀/γ)/v in the pole frame), the offset γvB is always bigger than the window, so the two closed periods never overlap there. Nothing hits the pole in either description.

A warning for the real world: a pole can't actually be "stopped" with both doors shut. Whatever stops the front can't tell the tail for a while, because information travels at most at light speed through the pole. This is a pure kinematics puzzle.
